AI Prompt:

You are an expert game designer and HR consultant specializing in gamified recruitment assessments. Your task is to design a complete, innovative online "game" for the first-round screening of graduate job applicants. The primary goal is to highly predictively screen for self-reliance: the ability to independently figure things out where information is available, without needing hand-holding, excessive guidance, or giving up when stuck. This game should simulate a realistic working environment in a way that's engaging but not overly arcade-like, filtering ~1000 applicants down to ~10 top candidates (when combined with a basic CV screen) who demonstrate strong self-directed problem-solving.

Key requirements for the game:

Target Audience: Recent graduates applying for entry-level roles (assume a general professional environment like tech, consulting, or business; make it adaptable).
Format: Web-based, accessible via browser or mobile app, lasting 20-45 minutes to minimize drop-off. Use simple tech like HTML5, JavaScript, or integrable with platforms like Articulate Storyline, Unity WebGL, or Google Forms with embeds for prototypes.
Core Mechanic: Create branching scenarios where information is deliberately scattered and incomplete. Candidates must proactively explore, research, and connect dots using in-game resources (e.g., virtual documents, links, or "hidden" tabs) without explicit tutorials or hints. Penalize or track behaviors like repeated "help" requests, stalling, or abandoning paths—reward persistence, creative navigation, and efficient discovery.
Predictive Elements for Self-Reliance:
Measure metrics like: time to first discovery, number of independent explorations vs. dead-ends hit, adaptability (e.g., pivoting without prompts), and completion rate without external aids.
Incorporate ambiguity: Vague initial briefs that mimic real work (e.g., "Handle this client issue" with no step-by-step guide).
Use behavioral analytics (e.g., via event tracking) to score traits like initiative (high score for voluntary resource dives) and resilience (low score for quick quits).
Make it predictive: Base on research from case studies like Unilever's Pymetrics games (neuroscience-based independence tests), Deloitte's virtual escape rooms (scattered info puzzles), Google's Foo.bar (self-research coding challenges), and PwC's simulation apps (hidden resource navigation). Aim for 25-30% better prediction of on-the-job autonomy than traditional screens, per Journal of Business and Psychology studies.
Creativity and Engagement: Be highly creative—infuse elements like narrative storytelling (e.g., a "day in the life" at a fictional company), subtle gamification (e.g., progress bars that unlock based on discoveries, not points), and realism (e.g., email inboxes, chat logs, or AI-simulated colleagues who give minimal responses). Include randomized elements to prevent cheating or memorization.
Inclusivity and Fairness: Ensure accessibility (e.g., keyboard navigation, alt text, no time pressures that disadvantage neurodiverse players). Reduce biases (e.g., no assumptions about prior knowledge; provide equal starting info). Include a post-game survey for feedback on fairness.
Scoring and Filtering: Automated system to rank candidates (e.g., 0-100 score, with thresholds: top 1% advance). Output data like heatmaps of exploration paths for HR review. Integrate with applicant tracking systems (e.g., export to CSV).
Implementation and Validation: Provide a step-by-step build guide, including tech stack, cost estimates (5K−20K for prototype), testing protocol (pilot on 50 diverse volunteers, measure correlation with self-reliance via follow-up surveys), and legal notes (e.g., GDPR compliance, bias audits).
Structure your output as a detailed blueprint:

Game Concept Overview: Title, high-level description, and objectives.
Detailed Scenarios: 3-5 interconnected levels or scenes, each testing escalating self-reliance (e.g., easy intro to hook, harder ambiguous challenges).
Mechanics and Rules: How players interact, what constitutes "success," and anti-hand-holding features.
Scoring System: Metrics, algorithms, and how it filters to ~10 candidates from 1000.
Creative Twists: Innovative elements to make it fun and memorable (e.g., AI-generated dynamic content).
Implementation Guide: Tools, timeline, and integration tips.
Potential Risks and Mitigations: Address drop-off, cheating, or false signals.
Sample Player Experience: A walkthrough narrative from a high vs. low self-reliance player's perspective.
Make this design innovative, practical, and laser-focused on self-reliance as the key signal. Output in clear, professional Markdown format with sections for easy reading.
